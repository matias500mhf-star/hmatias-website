Receiving a delivery is a control point, not a formality. Signing without inspection can turn a visible discrepancy into a difficult problem after unloading or use.

The procedure should be proportionate to the risk and type of item. A box of consumables and a technical machine do not require the same level of inspection.

## Before arrival

Have the purchase order or reference document, approved quotation, specification, quantities and acceptance criteria available. Confirm who will receive the goods, where unloading will take place and whether suitable handling equipment is available.

Where the item requires special transport, packaging or storage conditions, prepare the area before delivery.

## During inspection

Check item identity, reference, model, unit, quantity and packaging. Look for damage, moisture, deformation, broken seals or signs of poor handling.

For equipment, check serial numbers, accessories, manuals, warranties and required technical documents. Where functional inspection is necessary, it should be performed by a competent person under the applicable procedure.

## Record discrepancies instead of normalising them

For shortages, excess items, substitutions, damage or missing documents, create an objective record with the description, affected quantity, photographs and batch or unit identification. Do not change the approved specification or accept an informal substitution.

Notify the supplier and route the issue for a decision: correction, replacement, balance delivery, conditional acceptance or rejection, subject to contract and the relevant approval.

## Minimum delivery inspection record

| Field | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Reference | Purchase order or request |
| Item | Description and specification |
| Quantity | Ordered, received and accepted |
| Condition | Conforming, damaged or pending |
| Documents | Received and outstanding |
| Discrepancies | Description, photos and owner |
| Decision | Acceptance, reservation or rejection |

Receiving should end with a clear decision and an owner for each open action. Proper records protect both buyer and supplier, reduce disputes and support inventory control.
