The lowest quotation is not automatically the best proposal. A sound decision requires each quotation to answer the same requirement, include comparable items and meet the conditions under which delivery is expected.

A comparison should make differences visible. It is not simply a ranking of prices; it helps identify omissions, later costs and risks that may affect delivery.

## 1. Start with technical compliance

Before reviewing the amount, confirm that each proposal matches the requested specification. Check reference, brand or model where relevant, material, dimensions, grade, standard, capacity, finish and unit of supply.

Separate mandatory requirements from preferences. An alternative may be acceptable, but equivalence must be assessed against explicit criteria, not a general impression that two products look similar.

## 2. Normalise units and quantities

Compare prices on an equivalent unit basis. A quotation per box should not be compared directly with a quotation per item until both are converted to the same basis. Confirm pack sizes and minimum order quantities as well.

Record differences before calculating totals. Packaging can explain an apparent price gap.

## 3. Calculate the expected total cost

Show the product price, applicable taxes, transport, unloading, insurance, special packaging and other confirmed charges separately. Do not add assumed amounts: when a cost is not stated, mark it as pending confirmation.

The total should state its currency, calculation basis and assumptions.

## 4. Compare timing and commercial terms

Record lead time, dispatch location, quotation validity, payment terms, warranty and replacement conditions. A proposal valid for only a few days may require a different decision from one with a longer validity period.

Distinguish a supplier's stated lead time from a date already confirmed by a carrier or logistics operator.

## 5. Check documentation and responsiveness

Depending on the item, request a technical datasheet, certificate, declaration of conformity, manual, warranty, sample or batch identification. For critical supply, confirm how discrepancies and delays will be handled.

Documentation should relate to the product actually proposed, not merely to a broad product family.

## A simple comparison matrix

| Criterion | Control question |
|---|---|
| Compliance | Does it meet mandatory requirements? |
| Quantity | Are unit and packaging comparable? |
| Cost | Does the total include confirmed charges? |
| Timing | Is delivery compatible with the requirement? |
| Documentation | Is the required evidence available? |
| Terms | Are payment, warranty and validity clear? |

## 6. Record the decision

The recommendation should state the selected proposal, reasons, reservations and points still awaiting confirmation. Where a proposal is excluded, record the objective criterion it did not meet.

This improves traceability and prevents a decision from being justified solely by a price difference that may not survive through delivery.
