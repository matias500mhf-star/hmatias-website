Preventive maintenance starts with organised information about assets and operating conditions. Without an inventory, history and assigned owners, a calendar can become a list of tasks that does not reflect the actual risk of a facility.

A useful plan links each asset to a routine, evidence of completion and an escalation rule when an abnormal condition is found.

## 1. Build the asset register

Record asset ID, location, manufacturer, model, serial number where available, installation date, technical documents and internal owner. Consider electrical systems, air conditioning, pumps, fire protection, roofing, drainage and other equipment relevant to operations.

Do not assign an age or technical condition without evidence. Mark unknown information for survey.

## 2. Classify criticality

Assess the impact of failure on safety, business continuity, compliance, repair cost and equipment availability. The classification should reflect the real use of the facility, not simply the purchase value of the asset.

Critical assets may require more frequent checks, redundancy, spare parts or a specific response procedure.

## 3. Define tasks and intervals

Use manufacturer instructions, applicable legal requirements, environmental conditions and breakdown history. Describe what will be checked, by whom, with which tool and what evidence must be recorded.

Avoid arbitrary frequencies. An interval should have a clear basis and be reviewed when the equipment, usage or risk changes.

## 4. Assign responsibilities and schedule

Link each task to a date or window, owner, required resources and access conditions. Identify work that requires a qualified technician, authorisation, energy isolation or service interruption.

The calendar should distinguish planned maintenance, inspection, corrective repair and emergency response.

## 5. Record outcomes and anomalies

Each intervention should record the date, asset, tasks completed, observations, measurements where applicable, replaced parts and responsible person. Anomalies need a priority, an owner and a follow-up date.

Photographs help document condition, but they do not replace measurements or tests required by technical procedures.

## Useful management indicators

| Indicator | What it helps monitor |
|---|---|
| Planned tasks completed | Schedule compliance |
| Repeat failures | Problems not removed at source |
| Downtime | Operational impact |
| Emergency interventions | Pressure on corrective maintenance |
| Open actions | Decisions or resources still required |

The plan should be reviewed regularly. The objective is not to produce more paperwork; it is to reduce avoidable failures, improve predictability and retain enough evidence to make sound decisions.
